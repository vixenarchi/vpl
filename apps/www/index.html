How it is built

Six rules, kept because breaking them is how software gets fat.

  1. One account for the whole suite. Sign in once at any address and you are in all eight. Names ignore case; passwords never do.
  2. No bundler, no framework. A page is HTML, a stylesheet and a script, served from a CDN. There is nothing to compile and nothing to keep warm.
  3. Text is the truth. A diagram is a list of boxes in the document; the canvas edits the text. Notes, requirements and controls are markdown, so nothing is locked in a shape only we can read.
  4. The model proposes; a person decides. Nothing a model writes lands in a field on its own. It arrives in a speech balloon and waits for a button.
  5. Zero euro to start. The free tiers of a CDN and a database, and your own model key. There is no plan to upgrade to.
  6. No tracking. No analytics, no consent banner, no third-party scripts. What you write is stored so you can read it back, and deleting your account deletes it.

Who it is for

People who write things down for a living and are tired of tools that grow a dashboard every quarter: architects, analysts, auditors, small teams with one project and no administrator.

If you need roles, seats, single sign-on and an invoice in three currencies, this is not it — and that is on purpose.

What it costs

Nothing to start. The suite runs on free tiers; you bring a model key for the parts that use one, and vp sells credits if you would rather not bring your own.
